# P6 Deliverables: Methods §3.5 and Results §4.3

## Methods §3.5: M3 - Autoencoder

We implemented an autoencoder to detect network flows whose feature patterns deviate from the negative-class training population. The model reconstructs each 140-dimensional input, and its anomaly score is the mean squared reconstruction error across features. Larger errors indicate greater suspicion. Inputs use P2's delivered original stratified main dataset and fitted preprocessing, without generating new splits or refitting transformations. Only training rows with y_binary=0 enter model fitting. This follows P2's negative-class mapping rather than independently relabelling attempted flows.

Eight predefined configurations were evaluated with seeds 42–46, giving 40 fits. Candidates combined hidden widths of 64–16 or 128–32, learning rates of 0.001 or 0.0003, and weight decay of zero or 0.0001. The decoder mirrors the encoder, with ReLU hidden activations and a linear output. Adam used batches of 512, a maximum of 40 epochs, and early stopping after five epochs without a benign-validation MSE improvement exceeding 0.000001. Each fit retained its best checkpoint. Configuration selection minimized five-seed mean benign-validation MSE, with candidate identifier as the tie-breaker. Attack-labelled validation performance was not used for selection. <!-- Source: methods/m3/config.json; results/m3_runs/plan.json; results/m3_runs/search.json -->

For each selected checkpoint, thresholds were the 95th and 99th percentiles of reconstruction errors from 302,963 negative-class validation flows, using linear quantile interpolation. Each fit used 1,466,743 negative-class training flows. Both thresholds were frozen before test evaluation; error greater than or equal to the threshold indicated an attack. <!-- Source: docs/m3_threshold.md; results/m3_runs/search.json -->

Dense-layer inference scales with the sum of adjacent layer-width products; training additionally scales with sample count and epochs. CPU inference used P3's common batch benchmark. Isolation Forest and One-Class SVM were auxiliary comparisons, each fitted once to the same seed-42 subsample of 10,000 negative-class training flows and calibrated on all negative-class validation flows. Their training scope differs from the full-data autoencoder. Reconstruction quality does not guarantee attack separation, and the experiment does not independently establish generalization to unseen attack families. <!-- Source: methods/m3/run.py; results/m3_runs/records/*.json -->

## Results §4.3: M3 Results and Comparison

Configuration A06, with hidden widths 128–32, learning rate 0.0003 and zero weight decay, achieved the lowest mean benign-validation MSE of 0.005856. Its training runs stopped after 18–21 epochs; evaluation used each run's best validation checkpoint. On 346,544 test flows, including 64,038 attacks, the five-seed mean AP was 0.7427 ± 0.0062 and ROC-AUC was 0.9040 ± 0.0031. All reported dispersions are sample standard deviations across five seeds. AP and ROC-AUC are identical between threshold versions because the underlying anomaly scores are unchanged. <!-- Source: results/m3_runs/search.json; results/m3.json -->

At the 95th-percentile threshold, mean attack recall was 61.44% ± 1.31 percentage points and FPR was 4.84% ± 0.17 percentage points. Raising the threshold to the 99th percentile reduced FPR to 0.83% ± 0.14 percentage points, while recall fell to 52.27% ± 1.80 percentage points. Precision increased from 74.20% to 93.47%, and macro-F1 increased from 0.8028 to 0.8074. These are test operating rates at validation-calibrated thresholds, not thresholds selected from test ROC curves. <!-- Source: results/m3.json -->

Detection varied substantially by attack family. At the 95th-percentile threshold, mean recall was 94.26% for DoS Slowhttptest and 90.91% for DoS Hulk, but 10.32% for botnet, 4.43% for portscan and 0% for SQL injection. Heartbleed and infiltration each reached 100%, but their test supports were only two and five flows, limiting interpretation. <!-- Source: results/m3_runs/records/Autoencoder_seed*_q95.json -->

Auxiliary AP values were 0.5828 for Isolation Forest and 0.5046 for One-Class SVM; these are single-fit observations, not five-seed estimates. Selected autoencoder fits took 347.54 ± 36.62 seconds on average, including validation-based checkpoint selection. Mean amortized CPU inference cost was 0.001746 milliseconds per flow. This is batch-based cost, not single-request latency. Overall, the results show a measurable false-alarm–recall trade-off and substantial attack-family blind spots; they do not establish superiority over supervised methods without aligned evaluation. <!-- Source: results/m3_runs/auxiliary.json; results/m3_runs/records/Autoencoder_seed*_q95.json -->
