# P7 Results 4.4 and Discussion

## Results 4.4 Cross method comparison and dataset sensitivity

We evaluated GBDT, 1D-CNN and autoencoder models on original and corrected stratified Test splits containing 346,544 and 314,577 flows, respectively. Results report means and sample standard deviations across seeds 42–46. Corrected runs used commit 3632afa with the prepared configurations. GBDT retained G05, CNN retained its fixed configuration, and the autoencoder repeated its eight-candidate validation search, selecting A05 instead of original A06. Validation determined the decision thresholds. <!-- Sources: results/p7_corrected_summary/receipts.json; results/p7_corrected_summary/m3_search.json; results/p7_figure_data.json -->

GBDT achieved the highest AP and ROC-AUC on both versions. Original AP was 0.999495 ± 0.000073, compared with 0.917158 ± 0.023260 for CNN and 0.742710 ± 0.006170 for the autoencoder. Corrected AP was 0.99999985 ± 0.00000002, 0.986249 ± 0.018231 and 0.866415 ± 0.013134, respectively. Corrected ROC-AUC was 0.99999996, 0.997791 and 0.979057. AP increased by 0.0505, 6.9091 and 12.3705 percentage points. The ordering GBDT > CNN > autoencoder persisted for both ranking metrics (Figure 1). <!-- Sources: results/summary.csv; figures/fig_p7_overall_performance.png -->

Sensitivity depended on the operating point. CNN TPR@1% FPR increased from 0.611478 to 0.941949, while autoencoder TPR decreased from 0.527228 to 0.183953 despite its higher AP. At 5% FPR, their TPR values increased from 0.795075 to 0.999861 and from 0.617721 to 0.953509. Corrected GBDT achieved TPR 1.0 at both targets. Thus, the ranking persisted while the autoencoder changed most in absolute terms for AP, ROC-AUC and both TPR targets (Figures 2 and 4). These ROC operating points describe Test score distributions. Classification summaries separately apply the validation-frozen thresholds. <!-- Sources: results/p7_plot_values.csv; results/summary.csv -->

Family recall showed uneven changes under the frozen classification rules. CNN portscan recall rose from 0.1937 to 0.8845 and botnet recall from 0.0048 to 0.5673. Autoencoder DoS Hulk recall fell from 0.8436 to 0.2438, while infiltration recall rose from zero to 0.8. Corrected GBDT recalled every represented attack family. Corrected Heartbleed, infiltration and SQL injection supports were only 2, 5 and 2 flows, limiting interpretation of those recalls (Figure 3). <!-- Sources: results/p7_figure_data.json; figures/fig_p7_family_recall.png -->

Corrected CNN seed 43 had AP 0.954448 and TPR@1% FPR 0.745744, contributing to substantial seed variability. At the autoencoder q99 rule, mean Test FPR was 0.011425 and TPR was 0.260859. Changes in prevalence, family composition and autoencoder selection accompany the version comparison, so the observed differences describe the complete rerun pipeline. <!-- Sources: results/m2_corrected.json; results/m3_corrected.json; results/summary.csv -->

The auxiliary random forest achieved corrected AP 0.999995 and ROC-AUC 0.999998. Its results are retained separately in the summary. GBDT and CNN corrected Macro-F1 were 0.989868 and 0.965244, compared with 0.640824 for the autoencoder under the respective frozen classification rules. <!-- Sources: results/summary.csv -->

## Discussion

GBDT remained the strongest model under this stratified tabular evaluation. Its performance is consistent with supervised trees exploiting nonlinear relationships among engineered flow features. The experiments evaluate the delivered methods with their documented training procedures. CNN used one fixed configuration, while the original GBDT and both autoencoder searches considered eight candidates. The missing CNN search evidence limits conclusions about performance under equal tuning budgets. <!-- Sources: results/m1_gbdt_summary/summary.json; results/p7_corrected_summary/m3_search.json; methods/p7/m2_corrected.yaml -->

The autoencoder's higher corrected AP and ROC-AUC coexisted with lower TPR at the strictest false-alarm target. Global ranking improvement therefore gives insufficient information about coverage within a small alert budget. At 5% FPR, corrected autoencoder TPR reached 0.9535, whereas the 1% target yielded 0.1840. This difference makes the acceptable false-alarm rate central to operational interpretation. Validation-calibrated thresholds also have their own realized Test rates, reflecting differences between validation and test score distributions. <!-- Sources: results/summary.csv; results/p7_plot_values.csv -->

Version effects involve changes in the evaluated populations. Attack prevalence rose from approximately 18.48% to 24.61%, and portscan support changed from 13,854 to 36,241 flows. AP depends on prevalence, while family composition affects aggregate recall. Autoencoder selection also changed from A06 to A05 under the unchanged candidate grid and stopping rule. Consequently, these results measure sensitivity to the dataset and selection pipeline together. Establishing a specific causal effect of corrected labels would require matched populations and additional controls. <!-- Sources: results/p7_figure_data.json; results/p7_corrected_summary/m3_search.json; results/m3_summary/search.json -->

Five seeds exposed variability that a single run would conceal, particularly for CNN seed 43 and corrected autoencoder q99 recall. Rare-family estimates remain sensitive to individual flows. Near-perfect tree metrics on this split provide limited evidence about performance on future traffic, other networks or unseen attack families. Temporal and external validation would address those questions more directly. <!-- Sources: results/m2_corrected.json; results/m3_corrected.json; results/p7_figure_data.json -->

The autoencoder trained on benign examples and used attack labels during evaluation. This learning setup helps explain why its comparison with supervised detectors requires care. Low reconstruction error can occur for attacks that resemble benign feature patterns, leaving limited detection coverage at a strict threshold. <!-- Sources: methods/m3/run.py; results/m3_corrected.json -->

Reproducibility rests on saved configurations, the frozen training commit, file hashes, stage receipts and complete seed records. M2's base export omits per-row scores, limiting independent row-order verification and cross-method paired resampling. Previously documented original M2 operating-point tie handling also qualifies strict comparison with P3 exports. Figure 1 uses AP and ROC-AUC because complete PR arrays are unavailable in the common public summaries. Timing scopes and hardware differ across versions, and corrected M1 inference latency is unmeasured. These limits support reporting detection performance separately from controlled efficiency claims. <!-- Sources: results/p7_acceptance.json; docs/p6_review_20261110.md; results/summary.csv; docs/p7_results_and_handover.md -->
